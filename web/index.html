ETF RETURN LEADERS ● …

Who earned the most on ETFs (funds that trade like a stock and hold many investments) over the last 1, 3 or 5 years, and did they beat simply buying the S&P 500? Three groups, each measured as well as its public reports allow: fund managers (exact), big investors from SEC filings (approximate) and members of Congress (estimated). The groups are measured differently, so compare people within a group, not across groups.

NOT ENOUGH DATA (0)

    METHODOLOGY

    Every number is the time-weighted return of the person's ETF holdings with dividends reinvested, over the last 1, 3 or 5 years ending at the latest price. 3Y and 5Y also show the yearly (annualized) rate. "vs SPX" is the difference from SPY's total return over the same window, in percentage points.

    EXACT · Fund managers. The plain average of the market-price total returns of the ETFs they manage. A fund counts only if they have managed it for the whole window; a tenure given only as a year counts from 31 December of that year. The list is hand-checked against each fund's prospectus and is not every ETF manager. Funds that closed or merged have no price history for the whole window and do not count, which can make the group look better than it was. The return method is tested against the published market-price returns of SPY, QQQ and ARKK (within 0.1 percentage points).

    APPROX · Big investors (13F). Quarter-end ETF share counts from SEC Form 13F, held unchanged until the next report; trades between quarter-ends are invisible. Options and bonds are excluded. Listed only with at least $10M in ETFs at every quarter-end in the window. "% BOOK" is the ETF value as the filer reported it, divided by the reported value of all share positions in the same filing (latest quarter; hover for the average over the window): the return is that of the ETF part of the book only. By default the board hides filers whose ETFs are under 10% of their book. Big investors often hold ETFs to hedge or to park cash rather than as a bet, so a 13F return describes their ETF positions, not their skill as investors.

    EST · Congress. Holdings rebuilt from House and Senate trade reports since 2012, each trade sized at the midpoint of its reported range. The range next to each estimate shows the result if every trade were at the low end or the high end of its range. Ranked only if invested on at least 95% of trading days. Members may report up to 45 days late. Members report only while in office, so holdings end when a member leaves: someone who left during a window is listed under "Not enough data", and someone who left before it is not listed.

    BASIS shows what a row rests on: the number of funds, quarterly reports or trades, and the share of their value that could be priced (the lowest quarter for 13F; purchase amounts for Congress). A row is ranked only if at least 95% is priced. Beat the S&P counts people ranked in all three windows who beat SPY in every one: with thousands of people, some will top a single window by luck.

    Anyone who cannot be measured fairly is listed under "Not enough data" with the reason. Anything skipped or doubtful is listed under issues, with a link to the original document.

    Not investment advice. Congressional reports may be used only for lawful, non-commercial purposes. EXACT = from public fund prices · APPROX = from quarter-end snapshots · EST = from reported ranges. Found a wrong number? Report it on GitHub issues with the name and window.
